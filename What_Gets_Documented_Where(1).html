What gets documented where

Four primary homes. Each type of documentation has one clear primary location, with references between them where needed — the control description lives in the record and is referenced by the workpaper, not retyped into it. Most of the confusion in the new format is not about what to write; it is about which of these four it belongs in.

Home 1

Process Understanding

Workstep · pilot: separate Word doc
  • End-to-end process narrative — how work flows, systems touched, hand-offs
  • The process walkthrough itself
  • Coverage questions — e.g. how G&C knows every Gap was referred
  • Anything true of the process rather than of one control
Not hereJudgments about whether a control is well designed.
Home 2

Key Control record

Optro · the RCM row
  • Control description — one control per record
  • Control objective
  • Risk linkage, frequency, type, owner
  • The 5Ws & H answers — who performs it, what the criteria are, when it triggers
Not hereThe evaluation of whether those answers are good enough.
Home 3

Control Test Workpaper

Excel · one per control
  • Section 3 — one instance walked, and the key attributes
  • Section 4 — the evaluation of the 5Ws & H answers
  • Sections 5 & 9 — justification for the conclusions
  • Sections 7–8 — population, sample, testing attributes, results
  • Evidence tab — marked-up screenprints, tickmarked
Not hereThe conclusion words Effective / Ineffective.
Home 4

The workstep

Optro native fields
  • Design conclusion — Effective / Ineffective

The two splits people get wrong

Both of these sit on the boundary between Home 2 and Home 3, and both are stated in the template's guidance boxes — just never in one place.

The contentAnswers live inEvaluation lives in
The 5Ws & H Key Control record. Who performs the control, what is assessed and against which criteria, when it triggers, where it sits, how it is evidenced. These are facts about the control. Workpaper Section 4, Assessment Rationale. Whether those answers are sufficient given the risk. This is judgment, and it is the actual work of design testing.
The conclusion The workstep. Effective or Ineffective, design and operating effectiveness separately. Nothing else is a valid design conclusion — there is no "partially effective." Workpaper Sections 5 and 9. Why that conclusion holds, including why any Fail was overridden or why exceptions still support an effective conclusion.
The process Process Understanding workstep (pilot: attached Word document). Referenced from Section 3 of the workpaper by link, not restated. Nowhere in the workpaper. Section 3 records one instance of one control. If you are writing paragraphs about how the process works, the content is in the wrong home.
WHYRisk linkage
WHOPerformer — authority, SoD, override
WHATThe activity — criteria and thresholds
WHEREPlacement — right point in the process
WHENCadence or trigger
HOWInformation and evidence — reperformance

WHERE is placement, not evidence location. Where the evidence lives and whether someone could reperform from it is HOW. Swapping those two is the most common error in a first draft.

How test steps get written now

The attributes are the test steps. You write them once in Section 3 from the control description, they carry automatically to Section 8 for operating effectiveness, and they carry to the evidence tab as tickmarks. There is no separate paragraph of test-step verbiage to argue about — the Testing Overview states the intent of the test in two or three sentences, and the attributes do the rest.

Too broad to test
Specific enough to test
Gap was appropriately assessed and the resulting determination was reasonable and supported.
The assessment documents the applicable enterprise requirement criteria — [policy, version] — that were applied in reaching the determination.
Review and challenge was performed in accordance with enterprise requirements.
Required review and challenge is performed by [reviewer role] before the determination is finalized, and the reviewer's conclusion is evidenced.
Information recorded in the system of record was complete, accurate and timely.
The determination is recorded in [system] within [required timeframe] of finalization.
The recorded information agrees to the Gap Intake Form, supporting documentation and review outcome.
All [required fields] are complete.
The test for an attribute: could a colleague on another team test it without asking you what it means? If the answer is no, the missing piece is almost always a named criterion, a named role, a named system, or a named timeframe — the four things in brackets above. Those brackets are not placeholders to be tidied up later. They are the agenda for the walkthrough.
Why one broad step became three "Complete, accurate and timely" is three different failures with three different causes, and a single C or X in the sample grid cannot tell a reviewer which one occurred. Splitting them is not more testing — it is the same testing, marked in a way that survives review.

One control per workpaper

This is the constraint that makes everything above work. One design conclusion cannot cover an assessment that was right and a system entry that was late, so if the control record describes several activities, the workpaper cannot conclude cleanly on any of them.

The boundary has to be settled before the workpaper is finalized and testing is executed — not before planning starts. Build the RCM row during planning with a preliminary control hypothesis and the unresolved facts labelled, then refine it, or split it, once the walkthrough establishes what the process actually does. A row that changes after the walkthrough is the process working, not a planning error.

Splitting means testing less per workpaper, not more testing overall. The population and sample can be shared: validate once, reference the workstep from the second control, and complete only the testing attributes and results there. What cannot be shared is the conclusion. Each control gets its own design conclusion, its own operating-effectiveness conclusion, and any issue is raised against the specific control it affects.
