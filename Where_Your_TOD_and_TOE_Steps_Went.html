Where your TOD and TOE steps went

The two halves move differently, and that is the part worth getting straight. TOE steps become testing attributes. TOD steps do not — they become 5Ws & H content, with the answers in the control record and the evaluation in Section 4 of the workpaper.

TOD steps

Your TOD steps describe what the design has to be. In the new format you no longer write a paragraph concluding on each one. Each becomes a design dimension: the answer sits in the Key Control record, and whether that answer is good enough is the Assessment Rationale in Section 4. C2 marks the ones that move to the recording control if the split holds.

Existing TOD stepBecomes which dimensionWhat you now write, and where
TOD 1 — Receives potential Gaps identified by the BU WHENWHO Record: the trigger is referral of a Gap; the referring and receiving roles. Section 4: whether an event-driven trigger is clear and comprehensive. Not an attribute — testing one sampled Gap cannot show that every Gap was referred. That completeness question goes to Process Understanding.
TOD 1 — Assesses potential Gaps against applicable enterprise requirements WHATWHY Record: which policy and version, and the thresholds separating Issue, ORE and neither. Section 4: whether those criteria are written down well enough that two people reach the same determination, and whether the control addresses the risk.
TOD 1 — Coordinates applicable review and challenge WHOWHENHOW Record: reviewer role, authority to overturn, required timing. Section 4: whether the reviewer is independent of the assessor, and whether the challenge leaves a retrievable artefact rather than a conversation.
TOD 1 — Records applicable information and determinations in the system of record C2 WHATWHENWHEREHOW Record: system, required fields, recording deadline. Section 4: whether a deadline exists at all, and whether a finalization timestamp exists to measure against.
TOD 1 — Handles Gaps that do not meet Issue or ORE criteria WHATWHERE Record: the criteria for a "neither" conclusion and how it is documented. Section 4: placement — this is the one outcome nothing downstream ever re-examines, which is why the control has to be preventive here.
TOD 2.1 — Mechanism supports consistent assessment and determination WHATWHY Same dimensions as TOD 1's assessment step. The old design criterion and the old walkthrough step were asking the same question from two directions; they now meet in one rationale.
TOD 2.2 — Mechanism provides for applicable review and challenge WHOHOW Section 4 rationale on reviewer independence and evidence. If another function owns the review, this moves to a separate control with its own Section 4.
TOD 2.3 — Mechanism supports complete, accurate and timely recording C2 WHATWHENHOW Section 4 of the recording workpaper. If recording turns out to be one integrated act with the assessment, it stays in the single control's Section 4 instead.
TOD 2.4 — Mechanism supports and documents "neither" determinations WHAT Folded into the WHAT rationale rather than concluded separately — "neither" is one outcome of the same determination, not a second mechanism.
TOD 2.5 — Gap Intake Form captures sufficient information WHATHOW Absorbed. No standalone conclusion that the form is "sufficient." WHAT asks whether the form requires the criteria and rationale rather than just the conclusion; HOW asks whether that is enough to reperform from. The attributes then show whether it actually produced them.
The check: if one of your TOD steps turned into a testing attribute, look again — you have probably duplicated something already on the TOE side. TOD asks whether the design would work when performed correctly. TOE asks whether it was performed correctly.

TOE steps

These become the attributes, written once in Section 3 and carried into Section 8. The third column is the part that matters most: what had to be named to make each one executable by someone other than you.

Existing TOE stepBecomesWhat had to be named
TOE 2a — Inspect the completed Gap Intake Form and supporting documentation against applicable enterprise requirements and determine whether the Gap was appropriately assessed and the resulting determination was reasonable and supported A1 A completed Gap Intake Form exists for the referred Gap.

A2 The assessment documents the applicable enterprise criteria [policy, version] applied.

A3 The determination — Issue, ORE or neither — is supported by the assessment and documentation inspected.

A4 Where neither, the basis is documented. 		Which policy and which version. "Applicable enterprise requirements" is the whole criterion for A2, and it is currently unnamed.

"Appropriately" and "reasonable" were dropped rather than replaced — neither is checkable. "Supported" survives because it names what the support is compared to.

A4 came from the TOD side, not the TOE. It is N/A on most instances, so the sample has to be selected to exercise it.
TOE 2b — Verify that applicable review and challenge was performed over the assessment and resulting determination in accordance with enterprise requirements A5 The required review and challenge is performed by [reviewer role] before the determination is finalized, and the reviewer's conclusion is evidenced. The reviewer role, the timing, and what counts as evidence. "Applicable" and "in accordance with enterprise requirements" were carrying all three of those unstated.
TOE 2c — Compare the information recorded in the system of record to the Gap Intake Form and supporting documentation to determine whether it was complete, accurate and timely C2 A1 Recorded in [system] within [required timeframe] of finalization.

A2 The recorded information agrees to the form, the supporting documentation and the review outcome.

A3 All [required fields] are complete. 		The system, the timeframe, and the field list. Without a timeframe, "timely" has no criterion. Without a field list, "complete" has nothing to be complete against.

The review outcome was added as a comparison point; the old step did not mention it.
Why 2c became three and 2b stayed one. Complete, accurate and timely fail for different reasons and are proved by different evidence, and a single mark tells a reviewer that something failed without saying which. Right reviewer, right time, evidenced are conditions of a single act — remove any one and the review did not happen — so they stay together as one mark.
The brackets are the point Every [policy, version], [reviewer role], [required timeframe] and [required fields] above is a fact the current documentation does not contain. They are not formatting to tidy up later — they are the walkthrough agenda, and they are the difference between a test step that reads well and one two auditors execute the same way.
